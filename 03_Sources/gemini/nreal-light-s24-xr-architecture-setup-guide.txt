Nreal Light + Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra: Real-Time Multimodal XR Architecture & Setup Guide
This document compiles the complete hardware, software, and systems architecture for running Nreal Light (XREAL Light) on a Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra in real-time AR, VR, and XR workloads—integrating OpenCode Mobile, a custom multi-pane UI, Neurosity Crown EEG, Samsung Galaxy Watch Ultra biometrics, and NVIDIA DeepStream.
1. Hardware & Physical Architecture
1.1 Composite USB-C Interfaces
When tethered directly to the Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra (Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode), the Nreal Light acts as a composite USB-C hub running four concurrent hardware interfaces without requiring the legacy Nreal Computing Unit or an XREAL Beam:
1. DisplayPort Alt Mode Sink: Uncompressed 1920×1080 (2D) or 3840×1080 (Full Side-by-Side 3D) video at 60Hz driven directly by the Adreno GPU.
2. USB HID Sensor Interface: 1000Hz accelerometer, gyroscope, and magnetometer IMU packets.
3. Dual Grayscale SLAM Cameras: Two corner-mounted wide-angle sensors providing inside-out 6DoF visual-inertial odometry.
4. Center RGB Camera (UVC): A 5MP forward-facing USB Video Class camera for passthrough computer vision.
5. USB Audio Class: Stereo temple speakers and dual beamforming microphones.
1.2 Power, Thermal, and Optical Considerations
* Charge-and-Play Splitting: Driving micro-OLED displays alongside SLAM/UVC cameras and network streams drains roughly 25–35% battery per hour. Use a USB-C PD charge-and-play splitter (e.g., XREAL Hub or Viture USB-C adapter) that supports DP Alt Mode + USB data pass-through.
* Thermal Bypass: Enable Pause USB Power Delivery inside Samsung Game Booster settings during heavy GPU/streaming workloads so external power bypasses battery charging and reduces heat near the USB-C controller.
* Optical Transparency (#000000 Rule): Because the Nreal Light uses Birdbath micro-OLED optics, pure black pixels (#000000) emit zero light and become completely transparent to your eyes. Always design HUD elements on #000000 backgrounds and keep critical UI controls at least 40px inward from screen edges where birdbath optics soften.
* VR Light Blocker: Snap on the magnetic front light blocker for 3D SBS movies or VR streaming; remove it for optical see-through AR/HUD operation.
* Cable Care: The Nreal Light uses a non-detachable tether cable—avoid sharp bends near the temple hinge.
2. S24 Ultra OS Configuration & DeX Conflict Resolution
2.1 Resolving the Samsung DeX vs. Nebula Conflict
By default, plugging the Nreal Light into the S24 Ultra auto-launches Samsung DeX, which intercepts the USB display/HID interface and prevents XREAL's Nebula spatial runtime from initializing.
* To run Nebula (6DoF AR Space):
   1. Go to Settings > Connected devices > Samsung DeX and turn OFF Auto start when HDMI is connected.
   2. Sideload legacy Nebula for Android v3.8.0 or v3.8.1 (newer Play Store apps like MyGlasses / ControlGlasses dropped Nreal Light support).
   3. Grant Nebula Camera, Nearby Devices / USB, Display over other apps, and Location permissions, and set Battery usage to Unrestricted.
   4. Never lock the phone screen with the power button while inside Nebula AR Space (Android's ACTION_SCREEN_OFF broadcast suspends SLAM tracking); instead, use Nebula's in-app screen-dimming touchpad mode.

	3. Dual-Display Isolation: OpenCode Mobile + Custom Multi-Pane UI
To keep OpenCode Mobile interactive on your S24 Ultra's touchscreen while Samsung DeX displays your custom multi-pane UI inside the Nreal Light glasses:
1. Enable DeX Extend Mode: Connect the glasses, open Settings > Connected devices > Samsung DeX > Connected display, and select Extend (not Screen mirroring).
2. Disable Automatic Touchpad Takeover: Open Settings > Connected devices > Samsung DeX > Mouse and trackpad and turn OFF Auto open touchpad when DeX starts. Your S24 Ultra remains a normal touchscreen phone even without an external mouse.
3. Isolate OpenCode Mobile to the Phone (Display 0): Launch OpenCode Mobile exclusively on your S24 Ultra screen (configured with an OLED dark theme and connected over WireGuard/Tailscale to opencode serve). Do not launch it from the DeX app drawer, or Android will migrate its window into the glasses.
4. Launch Custom UI Exclusively in DeX (Display 1): Using a Bluetooth mouse/ring controller (or briefly tapping the DeX touchpad icon in the phone's navigation bar), open your custom UI inside Google Chrome as a borderless installed PWA (F11 maximized) and set Settings > Samsung DeX > Taskbar > Auto-hide taskbar to ON.
5. Prevent Link Hijacking: Set Samsung Internet or Firefox as the default browser on your phone screen so tapping links in OpenCode Mobile does not open tabs inside Chrome on DeX.
6. Lock Audio Routing: Use Quick Settings Media output (or Separate app sound) to route audio cleanly to the Nreal Light temple speakers while keeping microphone dictation active for OpenCode prompts.
4. Neurosity Crown Integration (@neurosity/sdk)
Because Android's built-in WebView lacks Web Bluetooth support, avoid embedding console.neurosity.co inside WebView wrappers. Instead, use @neurosity/sdk over Wi-Fi/Cloud WebSockets (or a backend relay) to stream processed cognitive metrics directly from the Crown's onboard processor:
* High-Signal Streams:
   * neurosity.focus() (~4 Hz): Cognitive load probability (0.0–1.0).
   * neurosity.calm() (~4 Hz): Calm/flow state probability (0.0–1.0).
   * neurosity.kinesis("commandLabel") (Event-driven): Trained motor-imagery triggers (e.g., leftHandPinch at probability > 0.85 with a 1000ms debounce) for hands-free OpenCode diff approvals or UI pane switching.
   * neurosity.signalQuality() (~1 Hz): Per-channel electrode contact status across all 8 dry sensors.
   * neurosity.status(): Connection state and battery level.
* Bandwidth Tip: Avoid streaming raw 8-channel 256Hz EEG (brainwaves("raw")) over mobile links alongside video/SSE streams; rely on onboard-computed .focus(), .calm(), and .kinesis() events.
Reference Transparent HUD Widget (TypeScript / React)
import React, { useEffect, useState } from "react";
import { Neurosity } from "@neurosity/sdk";

interface BciHudProps {
 deviceId: string;
 onKinesisTrigger?: (command: string) => void;
}

export const NeurosityHudWidget: React.FC<BciHudProps> = ({
 deviceId,
 onKinesisTrigger,
}) => {
 const [focus, setFocus] = useState<number>(0);
 const [calm, setCalm] = useState<number>(0);
 const [battery, setBattery] = useState<number | null>(null);
 const [status, setStatus] = useState<string>("offline");
 const [lastCommand, setLastCommand] = useState<string | null>(null);

 useEffect(() => {
   const neurosity = new Neurosity({ deviceId });

   const authSub = neurosity.onAuthStateChanged().subscribe((user) => {
     if (!user) setStatus("auth_required");
   });

   const statusSub = neurosity.status().subscribe((s) => {
     setStatus(s.state);
     setBattery(s.battery);
   });

   const focusSub = neurosity.focus().subscribe((f) => {
     setFocus(Math.round(f.probability * 100));
   });

   const calmSub = neurosity.calm().subscribe((c) => {
     setCalm(Math.round(c.probability * 100));
   });

   const kinesisSub = neurosity.kinesis("leftHandPinch").subscribe((intent) => {
     if (intent.probability > 0.85) {
       setLastCommand(intent.label);
       onKinesisTrigger?.(intent.label);
       setTimeout(() => setLastCommand(null), 1500);
     }
   });

   return () => {
     authSub.unsubscribe();
     statusSub.unsubscribe();
     focusSub.unsubscribe();
     calmSub.unsubscribe();
     kinesisSub.unsubscribe();
   };
 }, [deviceId, onKinesisTrigger]);

 return (
   <div
     style={{
       backgroundColor: "#000000",
       color: "#00E5FF",
       fontFamily: "monospace",
       padding: "12px 16px",
       border: "1px solid #1E293B",
       borderRadius: "6px",
       display: "flex",
       gap: "20px",
       alignItems: "center",
     }}
   >
     <div>
       <div style={{ fontSize: "11px", opacity: 0.7 }}>CROWN [{status.toUpperCase()}]</div>
       <div style={{ fontSize: "14px", color: "#00FF88" }}>
         BAT: {battery !== null ? `${battery}%` : "--"}
       </div>
     </div>

     <div>
       <div style={{ fontSize: "11px", opacity: 0.7 }}>FOCUS</div>
       <div style={{ fontSize: "20px", fontWeight: "bold", color: "#00FF88" }}>
         {focus}%
       </div>
     </div>

     <div>
       <div style={{ fontSize: "11px", opacity: 0.7 }}>CALM</div>
       <div style={{ fontSize: "20px", fontWeight: "bold", color: "#00E5FF" }}>
         {calm}%
       </div>
     </div>

     {lastCommand && (
       <div
         style={{
           padding: "4px 10px",
           backgroundColor: "#00FF88",
           color: "#000000",
           fontWeight: "bold",
           borderRadius: "4px",
         }}
       >
         TRIGGER: {lastCommand}
       </div>
     )}
   </div>
 );
};

5. Multimodal Fusion with NVIDIA DeepStream
When scaling up to capture live audio, video, Neurosity EEG, and Galaxy Watch Ultra biometrics simultaneously, use a centralized message broker (NATS JetStream or MQTT) and NVIDIA DeepStream as the synchronization spine rather than opening multiple direct WebSockets from the browser UI.
5.1 System Topology
[ Nreal Light ] ──(UVC RGB + Mic Audio via S24 Ultra)──> RTSP / WebRTC / SRT ──┐
                                                                              ▼
[ Galaxy Watch Ultra ] ──(Samsung Health Sensor SDK)──> Phone Relay ──> [ Broker (NATS / MQTT) ]
                                                                              │
[ Neurosity Crown ] ────(@neurosity/sdk Node/Go bridge)────────────────────────┘
                                                                              │
                  ┌──────────────────(Metadata Injection)─────────────────────┘
                  ▼
       [ NVIDIA DeepStream Pipeline ] (Jetson AGX / dGPU Node)
        ├── Video: nvurisrcbin -> nvinfer (Vision) ──┐
        ├── Audio: alsasrc/rtsp -> nvinferaudio ─────┼──> nvmsgconv + nvmsgbroker
        └── Biometrics/EEG: Pad Probe (NvDsUserMeta) ┘         │
                                                               ▼
                                               [ Custom UI / Nreal Light HUD ]

5.2 Ingestion & Synchronization Pipeline
1. Nreal Light Video & Audio (S24 Ultra → DeepStream):
   * Capture the center UVC RGB camera (libuvc / Camera2—remembering to request android.permission.CAMERA at runtime on Android 15/16+ before opening the USB device) and USB temple mic audio (AudioRecord).
   * Stream H.264/H.265 + Opus/AAC over WireGuard via RTSP/SRT/WebRTC into DeepStream's nvurisrcbin and nvinfer / nvinferaudio.
2. Galaxy Watch Ultra Biometrics (Watch → S24 Ultra → Broker):
   * Build a lightweight Wear OS background service using the Samsung Health Sensor SDK (v1.4.1+) subscribing to HEART_RATE_CONTINUOUS (HR + Inter-Beat Interval IBI for real-time HRV), SKIN_TEMPERATURE_CONTINUOUS, and ACCELEROMETER_CONTINUOUS.
   * Relay packets over Wear OS MessageClient / DataClient to the S24 Ultra, which immediately publishes them to telemetry.watch.biometrics.
3. Neurosity Crown EEG (Bridge → Broker):
   * Run a headless Node.js or Go worker publishing .focus(), .calm(), .signalQuality(), and .kinesis() events with NTP timestamps to telemetry.bci.crown.
4. DeepStream NTP Metadata Fusion (NvDsUserMeta):
   * Sync all devices (Watch, S24 Ultra, GPU node) to the same NTP time server and enable attach-sys-ts=1 in DeepStream's [streammux] config.
   * Use a GStreamer src pad probe right after nvinfer to match the nearest EEG and Watch biometric ring-buffer entries to NvDsFrameMeta.ntp_timestamp, attaching them as NvDsUserMeta.
5. Single Fused Stream to Nreal Light HUD:
   * Publish the unified JSON/FlatBuffers state via nvmsgconv + nvmsgbroker at 10–15 Hz to ui.hud.state.
   * Your Nreal Light custom UI subscribes to this single WebSocket feed—displaying synchronized HR/HRV, Focus/Calm, Kinesis triggers, and AI vision/audio events while automatically collapsing non-urgent notifications during high cognitive load (focus > 0.70).
